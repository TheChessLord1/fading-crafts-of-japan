How you can help

  • Individuals: buy fewer things, but choose ones that are well made and last. Ask who made them and where.
  • Schools: make a poster, invite a guest who knows a craft, or try a simple craft such as weaving or papier-mâché.
  • Communities: hold a craft fair, show crafts in a library, or invite makers to teach a workshop.

Useful resources and links

  • Kogei Japan: a guide to traditional crafts
  • kougeihin.jp: the official list of crafts (it was closed for maintenance in November 2026)
  • GO TOKYO crafts guide: craft workshops in Tokyo

Ideas and Actions

  • Buy directly from artisans so more of the money reaches them.
  • Try a craft workshop when you visit Japan, or watch artisans at work.
  • Visit a craft town, such as Inami, and stay in a hotel that supports local makers. (National Geographic, 2025)
  • Share one thing you learned here with a friend.
  • Choose a craft for your next school project.

Can crafts come back?

Yes! Satsuma kiriko, Minakuchi-zaiku, Ryukyu hariko and tamahagane all faded or disappeared and were later revived. (Google Arts & Culture, n.d.)

Each one returned because people cared enough to help.
