What is it?

Kijoka bashōfu is a light, thin cloth from Kijoka in northern Okinawa. It is woven from the fibres of a banana plant called itobashō. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

The fibres are hollow, so sweat can dry quickly. This keeps people cool in hot, humid weather. (OIST, 2022)

History

Banana-fibre cloth is said to have been made in Okinawa since around the 13th century. Royal families wore it, and later ordinary people did too. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

Production stopped during World War Two and started again after it ended. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

Why is it endangered?

Many of the makers are elderly and find the slow work difficult. There are more than 23 steps, all done carefully by hand. (OIST, 2022)

Fewer people grow the plant, so there is less fibre to use. (OIST, 2022)

How you can help

  • Learn about the research in Okinawa that is testing new ways to make the yarn. (OIST, 2022)
  • Visit Kijoka and respect local rules about taking photos.
  • Teach friends about it, because even many young people in Okinawa know little about this cloth. (OIST, 2022)

Quick facts

  • Made in Kijoka, Okinawa
  • Said to go back to about the 13th century
  • More than 23 steps
  • About 60 plants for one roll of cloth (Kogei Japan, n.d.)
