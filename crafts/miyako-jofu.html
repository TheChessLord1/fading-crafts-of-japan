What is it?

Miyako jōfu is a smooth, shiny cloth from Miyako Island in Okinawa. It is made from the ramie plant and dyed with Ryukyu indigo.

The threads are pulled from the plant by hand. The cloth lets air through and can last for three generations. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

History

Ramie cloth is believed to have been made on the island since the 15th century. From 1637, women had to make it to pay a tax to their rulers.

Production was highest from about 1912 to the late 1920s. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

Why is it endangered?

Production fell after World War Two. The work is also extremely slow: even an experienced weaver finishes only about 20 cm a day, and one roll takes more than three months to weave.

People in Okinawa are now working to train the next makers. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)

How you can help

  • If you visit Miyako Island, look for places where you can learn about the weaving.
  • Understand why handmade cloth costs more: one roll can take years from start to finish. (Kogei Japan, n.d.)
  • Share the story of the weavers at school.

Quick facts

  • Made on Miyako Island, Okinawa
  • Believed to date from the 15th century
  • About 20 cm woven in a day
  • Plant used: ramie
