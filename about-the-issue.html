What is the issue?

Many traditional Japanese crafts are in danger of disappearing. The people who make them are getting older, and not enough young people are learning the skills.

Craft expert Steve Beimel says Japan had more than 300,000 certified craftspeople in the early 1970s. By 2021 there were fewer than 50,000, and most were over 50 years old. (FCCJ, 2021)

Where does it happen?

It happens all over Japan. The government officially recognises 244 traditional crafts, as of 27 October 2025. (METI, 2025) Every prefecture has at least one. (Beart.world, 2025)

Some small towns depend on a craft. In Inami, Toyama, about 150 of roughly 8,000 residents are woodcarvers. (National Geographic, 2025)

Who is affected?

  • Artisans, who may earn less and have nobody to teach.
  • Craft towns and families, whose work and identity are linked to a craft.
  • Other makers. Many crafts need several specialists. If one stops, the whole process can break down. (FCCJ, 2021)
  • Everyone who enjoys culture, because skills and history may be lost.

What are the causes?

  • Mass production: after the war, factory-made goods reduced demand for handmade ones. (Zenbird, 2021)
  • No successors: many young people choose other careers that pay more. (Zenbird, 2021)
  • Changing lifestyles: people live differently, move to cities, and the population is shrinking. (National Geographic, 2025)
  • Less work to share: there may not be enough for both a master and an apprentice. (FCCJ, 2021)
